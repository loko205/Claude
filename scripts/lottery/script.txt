An old man spent thirty-six hundred dollars on lottery tickets, and prayed nobody would hit the jackpot.

"Grandpa, you know you only need one to win, right?"
He doesn't answer. He just stares at the jackpot sign.
Draw night, nobody hits the jackpot, and he walks out with sixty-three hundred dollars.
"Beginner's luck," says the kid.
So next time, he brings his wife, and they buy so many tickets the kid misses his lunch break.
"You two are gonna lose the house."
Wait, why would anyone want nobody to win?
You see, this game had one weird rule.
When the jackpot got huge and nobody won it, the money got split between everyone who matched just a few numbers.
It's like a birthday party where the birthday kid never shows up.
Nobody gets the whole cake, so everyone's slice gets way bigger.
And once every slice was worth more than a ticket, buying more tickets wasn't gambling. It was math.
For nine years, he and his wife only played on those nights, and won twenty-six million dollars.
And the kid who laughed printed every single ticket.
