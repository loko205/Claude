# Ricks Clips (@RicksClips3000) – format analysis (2026-11-25)

Data: all 155 shorts listed with yt-dlp (`shorts.txt`, `by_views.txt`), 13 top story shorts watched by Gemini
(`gemini/<id>.md`: transcript, structure, captions, visuals, pacing). Study material only – never copy scripts.

## 1. What the channel is
- Two formats. **Old:** one-gag sketch clips ("Which One Is It? 😭🤣", "Diglett 💀") – mostly 0.1–5 M views.
  **New:** narrated story/explainer shorts – they carry the channel: 35 M, 31 M, 19 M, 17 M, 16 M, 14 M, 13 M, 12 M,
  11 M, 10 M x4. We copy ONLY the new format.
- Titles: "The <Thing> That <did something>" · "Why <people> <did odd thing>" · "How <trick> Worked" ·
  "The <Person> Who <odd behaviour>". Never a date, a name, or a famous event.

## 2. Topics of the hits (two families, ~50/50)
- **Hidden physics/body mechanism behind an odd act:** seismograph jar (35 M), rail expansion gaps (31 M), storm
  oil on waves (17 M), clothoid loop (13 M), target fixation (12 M), pink cell (11 M), reaction vs decision (10 M).
- **Scheme / loophole / con:** Japanese "boredom room" (19 M), pachinko token window (16 M), faked death with grave
  bell (14 M), lottery sting on cashiers (10 M), furniture-moving buyer (10 M), mine salting (7 M).
- Setting is vague and timeless: "the emperor", "the West", "a canyon", "a jail", "Japan". At most one nickname
  ("Slow Eddie"). Characters are roles: the engineer, his boss, the captain, the guards.

## 3. Script formula (measured on 13 transcripts)
- **Length 46–65 s, 160–230 words.** Speech is dense, no pauses; ends on the last word.
- **Hook = sentence 1, 6–16 words, an absurd ACTION with the reason hidden.** 7/13 start with "He …":
  "He sawed gaps into a railroad every 39 feet." · "He poured cooking oil into the sea with a pirate ship …" ·
  "They buried a man alive with a bell tied to his finger." · "He loaded the shotgun with gold and fired it into the
  walls of the mine." Alternative: paradox as fact ("Gambling is illegal in Japan. Yet there's a gambling hall on
  almost every street.", "If you plant a tree beside an empty road, cars will steer right into it.").
- **"You see," is used TWICE:** once right after the hook (0:03–0:09) to slide into the setup ("You see, two
  companies are racing to build a railroad…"), and again later to open the real explanation. 9/13 use it.
- **Mocker = 1–3 short quoted lines, rising in cockiness**, from a boss/captain/guards/rival/officer:
  "You're wrecking my railroad!" → "See, even your gaps know you were wrong." ·
  "We're about to get boarded and you're making soup?" → pirates: "Why waste a cannonball when the storm will do it
  for free?" · "Barbie's gonna love it in here!" · "Hey, it's the toy guy. Did your frogs get hungry again?" ·
  "So, someone broke in to move your furniture an inch?" + "He laughs all the way back to his car."
  Hero never argues; at most 2–4 words ("Steer into the storm." / "Trust the oil.").
- **Escalation in 3 concrete steps with tiny numbers/images:** "In May, you can fit a coin in them. By July, barely
  a sheet of paper." · "At 15 minutes he's sitting quietly. At 20 he's pacing." · "Then one ball. Then another,
  then all eight at once."
- **Mystery beat** (6/13): "Wait, what?" · "Wait, practiced?" · "How is that possible?" · "So how does this keep
  happening?" – right before the explanation.
- **Explanation via something the viewer has done:** "Ever blown on a bowl of soup?" · "Think of the last time your
  phone slid off a table." · "A car can creep round a hairpin at five, but take it at 60 and it flips."
- **Ending = the visual payoff on the mocker, as ONE image, last sentence:** "…the perfect railroad kicks sideways
  like a snake right as a train rolls onto it." · "…it curls over and drops straight onto the pirates' deck." ·
  "Just as the rival's cart hits the bottom of that perfect circle." No moral, no outro, no "and that's why".
  Variant: the twist reveals the hero was the schemer ("…and the buyer is him.").
- Present tense, "he/you", contractions, sentences ~12 words, jokes = exaggerated images ("He'd lose a race to the
  saloon against a guy carrying two beers", "the tow truck guy starts parking out there with a lawn chair").

## 4. Captions (Gemini, 13/13 agree on the basics)
- 1–2 (max 3) words, hard swap per word group, small scale-up pop on entry.
- Heavy rounded sans (Montserrat Black / "The Bold Font" style), white, thick black outline (+ soft shadow).
- Lower-middle / centre of the frame (well above the Shorts UI).
- Key nouns/verbs in **yellow** (sometimes green/pink matching the topic). Some videos lowercase.
  (Gemini colour details are not 100 % reliable – verify on a frame when we can.)

## 5. Picture & sound (consistent with the skill's STYLE_BIBLE)
- Every sentence illustrated literally; light changes with the story (day → sunset → night-blue for the danger →
  morning for the reveal). Cutaway diagrams/x-ray views for the mechanism.
- Ball drops, clinks, impacts get an SFX; music shifts to low tension in the danger beat, sting on the payoff.
